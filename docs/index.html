Abstract

Everyday economic relationships frequently depend on agreements that are informal, partially documented, or represented only as static documents. This creates recurring problems of ambiguity, fragmented evidence, obligation tracking, and settlement. Social Contract proposes an open-source computational framework in which an agreement is represented as structured data with explicit parties, rights, obligations, conditions, events, evidence, signatures, and settlement rules.

The study begins in Bangladesh and uses agricultural landowner–cultivator crop-sharing as its pilot domain. The pilot is deliberately narrow: the objective is not to build an agricultural information system, but to investigate whether a real pre-existing economic transaction can be represented, executed, evidenced, and settled through a general contract model.

The legal analysis maps the proposed architecture against Bangladesh's Contract Act, Evidence Act, Stamp Act, Registration Act, Transfer of Property Act, State Acquisition and Tenancy Act, and the Evidence (Amendment) Act, 2022. Particular attention is given to contract formation, documentary proof, digital records, digital signatures, stamping, registration, and immovable-property relationships.

Central hypothesis. A structured digital representation of an agreement can reduce ambiguity, evidence fragmentation, and settlement friction compared with informal or document-only workflows.

1. Problem Statement

A contract is commonly treated as a document. In practice, a contract is a relationship that unfolds over time. Parties make promises, satisfy conditions, exchange money or goods, submit evidence, miss deadlines, amend terms, and eventually settle or dispute obligations.

Static documents are useful records, but they do not inherently answer:

  • Who owes what?
  • When does an obligation become active?
  • What evidence demonstrates performance?
  • Which obligations remain incomplete?
  • What happens if a condition is not met?
  • How should a variable settlement be calculated?
  • Which legal formalities apply to this contract type?

Social Contract treats these questions as computational objects while preserving a human-readable contractual representation.

2. Research Questions

  1. Can common real-world agreements be represented using a small reusable contract ontology?
  2. Can contractual obligations be represented as machine-readable states and transitions?
  3. Can evidence be associated directly with individual obligations and execution events?
  4. Can structured settlement rules reduce calculation ambiguity?
  5. Can jurisdiction-specific legal requirements be represented as explicit compliance rules?
  6. Can the same underlying contract model support multiple economic domains?
  7. Does structured contract execution produce measurable improvements over ordinary document exchange?

3. Methodology

The study follows a design-science and empirical-validation approach. A pre-existing transaction is first documented as a social and economic workflow. Relevant legal requirements are then mapped to computational primitives. A prototype implements those primitives and is evaluated against the original workflow.

Step 1 — Problem observationDocument how agreements are currently formed, performed, evidenced, and settled.
Step 2 — Legal mappingIdentify applicable statutory requirements and constraints.
Step 3 — OntologyDefine parties, obligations, rights, events, evidence, and settlement primitives.
Step 4 — PrototypeImplement the structured contract lifecycle and audit model.
Step 5 — PilotTest the system on Bangladesh agricultural crop-sharing agreements.
Step 6 — EvaluationMeasure ambiguity, execution time, evidence completeness, and settlement friction.

5. Bangladesh Pilot

Bangladesh is selected as the initial jurisdiction because the pilot can be grounded in a concrete economic relationship: an agricultural landowner provides land and a cultivator provides cultivation activity and associated inputs under an agreed arrangement.

A pilot contract may encode a crop, plot identifier, contribution responsibilities, harvest basis, allocation percentages, evidence requirements, settlement procedure, termination terms, and dispute procedure.

Important legal classification issue. A crop-sharing arrangement should not automatically be treated as a conventional lease. The legal characterization of a specific arrangement depends on its actual terms and applicable law. The Transfer of Property Act, 1882 defines a lease broadly in section 105 and expressly includes a share of crops among possible consideration, while section 107 imposes registration requirements for specified leases. This makes legal classification a research variable rather than a software assumption.

6. Bangladesh Legal Framework

Social Contract uses Bangladesh as its first pilot jurisdiction. The legal layer is intentionally modular: a contract can be represented computationally, but the system must also identify when statutory formalities such as writing, stamping, witnessing, registration, or special land-tenure rules may apply.

Compliance boundary. A digital contract interface does not automatically remove statutory requirements. Social Contract should identify applicable formalities and route the parties through the appropriate legal process; it should never claim that software alone makes an otherwise non-compliant instrument valid.

9. Limitations and Legal Disclaimer

This page is a research and engineering artifact. It is not legal advice, a legal opinion, or a guarantee that any generated agreement is enforceable.

Applicability depends on the facts, contract type, parties, property, consideration, execution method, stamping, witnessing, registration, sector-specific law, and amendments in force at the relevant time.

In particular, the pilot must distinguish crop-sharing, agricultural tenancy, lease, licence, service, partnership, and other legal relationships rather than assuming that all arrangements involving land are legally identical.

Before production deployment for consequential transactions, the legal mapping, templates, compliance rules, identity/signature method, and registration/stamping workflow should be reviewed by qualified Bangladesh legal professionals.

Earlier versions of this page used guessed section/chapter URL patterns. The Bangladesh Laws website uses several different URL forms for individual sections, and those guessed paths can fail. This version deliberately uses the stable act-details-*.html pages returned by the official portal and does not fabricate section URLs.
